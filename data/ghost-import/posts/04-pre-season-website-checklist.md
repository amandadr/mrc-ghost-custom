Visitors start looking before you feel ready. They check Saturday hours, whether you are open yet, and whether they can book, order, or inquire without calling.

If those answers are last year’s, they phone. Or they pick somewhere else.

This is not a redesign project. It is a list of facts and one next step, checked on a phone, before the season starts answering for you.

> If you only have ten minutes, confirm the opening date, weekend hours, and whether someone can take the next step on a phone. Then make Google say the same thing.

## If you only have ten minutes

1. Opening date — or a clear “closed until” line — on the page visitors actually land on.
2. Weekend hours for the first month you are open.
3. The next step on a phone: book, order, or inquire, without calling.
4. The same opening date, hours, and next step on your Google listing.

That is the whole checklist. Everything below is optional.

## When this work has to happen

Atlantic seasons do not wait for a quiet week in May.

**February and March** are for anything that needs a developer: a booking path that dies on a phone, a form that goes nowhere, a site nobody on staff can edit. That work is too late once you are hiring for opening weekend.

**April** is for facts. Hours, rates, menus, packages, “open for the season,” who answers the inbox. Seasonal staff should be able to change those in ten minutes.

**By the first long weekend in May**, the people who will book July are already looking. You can still fix a wrong hour. You cannot start a new website.

If you are reading this in the fall, that is a good time. Do the ten-minute version now so you know what is actually broken. Schedule the rest for late winter.

## Facts visitors need

Check these on a phone, not at a desk.

**Hours and opening date.** Winter hours, “see you in the spring,” and last year’s festival dates are the usual leftovers. If you are closed midweek in June, say so. A visitor who drives and finds a locked door will not try twice.

**The thing that changes every year.** A menu that only exists in July. A rate that went up. A package you are not running. A phone number that belonged to last season’s manager. Pick the three facts people asked about most last year and make those right first.

**Where the truth lives.** Pick one place that wins when there is a conflict — usually the website or the Google listing, whichever you will actually keep current once the line starts. Every other listing is a copy. If the copies disagree, [they are two different systems](/blog/website-and-google-listing/). Fix the copies. Do not add a fifth one.

## The next step

A correct hour is not enough if the next step fails.

Open the site on a phone. Try to book a table, a room, a ticket, or send an inquiry. If you cannot finish without calling, visitors will call — and you will not have the staff to answer the same five questions all day.

If booking lives on another site, that is fine. The website still has to get them there in one tap. A “Book now” that opens the wrong season, an old calendar, or a login nobody uses is a closed door with nicer type.

Do this once in April and once the week you open. Paths rot.

## Who can update it

Once the season is underway, you will not have time to file a ticket to change Saturday hours.

Write down, on one page:

- Who can change hours, rates, and “we are closed today.”
- Where they log in.
- Which page they edit — not “the website,” the actual page.
- Who owns the Google listing.

If the answer is “only the developer” or “only me, and I am on the line,” the site is not ready. The winning source of truth has to be something a seasonal hire can edit in ten minutes.

You do not need a full manual. You need those four lines, printed or saved where the next person will look.

## What you can skip

You do not need a new theme. You do not need every tourism directory updated on the same afternoon. You do not need a chatbot.

You do not need perfect photography of a room you have not cleaned yet. A current hour beats a new hero image.

You do not need to name a platform. If the booking tool you already have still takes a reservation on a phone, keep it. Replacing it in April is how you spend May explaining why the calendar is empty.

If you are not sure what is wrong, do the ten-minute version. The broken step usually announces itself.

## What you can ignore

- A redesign so you “look ready.”
- A ranking package before the hours match.
- Every directory at once. Start with your site and Google.
- A new booking engine the week before you open.
- The feeling that you should have started in January. Start with the four items at the top.

If visitors kept asking questions your site should have answered last season, those questions are the checklist.
