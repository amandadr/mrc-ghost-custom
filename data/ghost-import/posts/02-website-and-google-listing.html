A visitor searching from a phone does not think about your website and your Google listing as two systems. They think about Saturday hours, whether you are open, and whether it is worth the drive.

Those answers often live in different places. They drift.

A source of truth is the place where the current hours, rates, or menu actually live — and every other listing is copied from there.

Two sources, one visitor

Your website is the page you (or a developer) update. Your Google Business Profile is a separate listing Google shows in Maps and in search. They do not update each other unless someone makes them.

Add a booking widget, a Facebook page, and a tourism directory, and you can have four versions of the same Saturday in July.

When they disagree, people believe the one they saw first. Then they phone. Or they do not come.

This is not an SEO trick. It is a source-of-truth problem. If you do not have one, you will keep fixing the same mistake in three tools.

Google is how a lot of people find you. The website is the place you control the next step. They do different jobs. They still have to tell the same story about hours, location, and whether someone can book, order, or inquire without calling.

A familiar path

Someone looking up a place in eastern PEI for the weekend sees Google say open until eight. The website, last touched in the off-season, still shows winter hours. The Instagram bio says “see our story for hours.”

None of those people is trying to be difficult. The system has three clocks.

Tourism makes this worse because the facts change: opening day, shoulder season, storm closures, a menu that only exists in July. Staff cannot pause a line to update five platforms. Once the season is underway, the cost of a mismatch is a full inbox and a missed booking.

What to do this week

  1. Pick one place that wins when there is a conflict. For most operators that is either the website or the Google listing — whichever you will actually keep current during the season.
  2. Open the visitor path on a phone: hours, address, and whether they can book, order, or inquire without calling.
  3. Check whether that next step is the same in Google as it is on the site.
  4. Fix the copies that disagree. Do not start a redesign.

If seasonal staff will be the ones changing hours, the winning source has to be something they can edit in ten minutes. If only a developer can change it, it will be wrong by the first holiday.

What you can ignore

  • Paying for a ranking package before the hours match.
  • A new visual theme to “look more professional.”
  • Every directory at once. Start with Google and your own site.

If visitors keep asking questions your site should have answered, the listing and the site are probably telling two stories.
